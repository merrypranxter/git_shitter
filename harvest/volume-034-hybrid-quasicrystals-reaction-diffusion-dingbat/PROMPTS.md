## 01 — Phason-Phonon Lattice Blooms

**BASIS:** Reaction-diffusion systems are known for producing complex patterns like Turing spots or spiral waves. Quasicrystals, with their unique 5-fold or 8-fold symmetries and long-range order without periodicity, offer a novel scaffolding for these diffusion dynamics. The interaction can lead to patterns that are ordered yet never repeat, akin to the phason and phonon modes in quasicrystalline materials.

**PARENT CONTRIBUTIONS:**

- **quasicrystals:** Utilizes the Ammann-Beenker octagonal tiling (8-fold symmetry) as a geometric scaffold, influencing the spatial propagation and local arrangement of reaction-diffusion fronts. The concept of aperiodic order and long-range coherence is central.
- **reaction_diffusion:** Employs the principles of reaction-diffusion systems (activator-inhibitor dynamics) to generate complex, emergent patterns. The specific pattern types (e.g., Turing spots, spirals) are the visual output of these dynamic simulations.

**FUSION LOGIC:** The reaction-diffusion simulation's local chemical interactions are guided by the global, aperiodic structure of the quasicrystal. Instead of diffusing uniformly, the 'activator' and 'inhibitor' concentrations are channeled or influenced by the specific angles and local configurations dictated by the 8-fold symmetry of the Ammann-Beenker tiling, preventing the formation of a repeating unit cell and instead generating an ordered but non-periodic distribution of pattern features.

**IMAGE / IMPLEMENTATION PROMPT:**

The patterns should appear to 'grow' or 'bloom' along the specific, non-periodic symmetry lines of an 8-fold Ammann-Beenker tiling. Include generous spacing between glyphs. The overall aesthetic should evoke the emergent complexity of biological morphogenesis or mineral growth.

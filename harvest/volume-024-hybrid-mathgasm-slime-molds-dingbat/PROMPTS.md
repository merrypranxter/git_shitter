## 01 — Plasmodial Invariant Knot

**BASIS:** The recursive branching of slime mold plasmodia, combined with the concept of topological invariants in knot theory from Mathgasm.

**PARENT CONTRIBUTIONS:**

- **Mathgasm:** Leveraged the core idea of topological invariants, specifically knot theory, to define the essential structural property of the glyph: its interconnectedness and resistance to simplification without cutting.
- **slime_molds:** Modeled the visual form of the glyph on the reticulated, branching, and adaptive network morphology of slime mold plasmodia.

**FUSION LOGIC:** The slime mold's branching network provides the 'physical' structure and visual language for the knot, while Mathgasm's focus on topological invariants dictates the underlying mathematical 'rules' or properties that the knot must embody, making the knot's complexity and resilience mathematically meaningful.

**IMAGE / IMPLEMENTATION PROMPT:**

Create a Daddy Dingy dingbat sheet. The first glyph is a complex, self-intertwining knot structure, entirely black on white. The knot's primary form should mimic the dense, vein-like network of a slime mold's plasmodium. Incorporate subtle details that suggest topological invariants, such as crossings that are clearly defined or 'smoothable' regions that imply a continuous deformation. Ensure generous spacing around the glyph. The sheet must contain exactly nine distinct glyphs, isolated, black-on-white, in a 3x3 grid.

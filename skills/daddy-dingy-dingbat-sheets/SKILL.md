# Daddy Dingy Dingbat Sheet Skill

## Role and objective

You create Daddy Dingy dingbat sheets: black-on-white source artwork intended to be separated into individual glyphs, traced into vectors, used as dingbats, and decorated later in Sparkle Bae with colored fills, glitter, metallic or other finishes, and optional multiple outlines.

**Nonnegotiable guiding idea: composition over complexity.** Intricate details frequently trace and decorate beautifully. Do not assume fine linework, dots, stippling, or ornamentation are forbidden. Focus instead on whether the design remains legible when all black portions share one fill treatment.

## Default output contract

Unless the user explicitly overrides a setting, make one image with:

- **Canvas:** 1:1 square.
- **Background:** flat, pure white (#FFFFFF).
- **Artwork:** pure solid black (#000000) only; internal open areas remain white.
- **Count:** exactly nine different dingbats.
- **Arrangement:** three columns by three rows, with the grid entirely invisible.
- **Separateness:** nine independent visual islands, each extractable as its own dingbat.
- **No extras:** no printed captions, numbers, lettering, visible boxes, guides, borders, cell lines, watermarks, or labels unless expressly requested as part of a design.

## Critical extraction-safe spacing rule

Do not fill each grid cell to its edges. Make each glyph a little smaller than it could be, and leave generous uninterrupted white clearance on all four sides, including diagonally. Maintain clearly visible whitespace between rows **AND** between columns. A reliable starting composition is for each glyph’s overall bounding area, including its dots/drips/flourishes, to occupy roughly 65–75% of its imaginary cell in its dominant dimension. This is a guide, not a rigid sizing limit; preserve clarity and spacing above all.

Avoid a visual read of “three rows of connected ornaments,” “three grouped columns,” or a tightly packed sheet. Daddy Dingy may treat neighboring graphics, whole rows, or whole columns as a single object if they are too close. The white gap must remain empty, not dotted with stray marks. All nine should be substantial and usable—not tiny—but comfortably isolated. Check the widest flourish and the last dot, not just the central body.

## Black and white: intended later behavior

- **BLACK = primary decoratable material.** Black silhouettes, outlined shapes, thin black linework, spots, engraving-like detail, and textured black marks will become the material receiving a shared fill or finish in the downstream workflow. Anything the user specifically wants glittered or filled should be represented by black.
- **WHITE = background / negative space.** White cutouts, interior slots, facet divisions, open channels, deliberate holes, and whitespace separate forms and reveal structure. White isn’t intended as a separate colored paint region.
- **OUTLINES = downstream effect.** Sparkle Bae may add more than one outline. Internal contours can help forms remain distinguishable. Do not assume outlines themselves can receive the same finishes as the main black fill, and don’t require outlines to rescue an unreadable composition.
- **FINISHES = downstream effect.** Do not paint in simulated glitter, chrome, gradients, colored foil, or glossy photographic lighting in the source sheet. Those come later.

## Rendering and detail

**Allowed and encouraged when appropriate:**

- Broad black-filled shapes and bold silhouettes.
- Delicate but readable black linework and internal contouring.
- White cutouts, channels, faceting, voids, and repeated holes.
- High-detail stippling, black-only engraving-style texture, patterned surfaces.
- Detached dots, drops, stars, tendrils, curled flourishes, ornamental motifs.
- Odd proportions, asymmetry, recursive mathematics, fractals, alien anatomy, psychedelic decoration.

**Not allowed by default:**

- Gray tones, gradients, colored areas, colored backgrounds, drop shadows, semi-transparent shading, photographic tones, soft blur, or simulated glitter.
- Visible layout/grid borders or background panels.
- Shapes belonging to adjacent dingbats that touch, connect, overlap, or sit so close they group visually.

Do not turn “vector-friendly” into “minimalist.” The detailed references demonstrate that complexity can work exceptionally well. Black-and-white illustration, including rich mark-making, is acceptable when the composition survives a unified fill.

## Composition, not simplicity

Each glyph needs an intentionally organized focal point and visual hierarchy. Use silhouette, spacing, black contouring, white-negative-space channels, and recognizable structure so important elements remain distinguishable even when every black pixel receives the same one-color/glitter finish.

The central test:

> **If all black areas become the same color or glitter material, does this design still look good, and can I tell its important parts apart?**

A complex alien head may work better than a simple scene with a figure merging into a cave. The problem in the second case is not line count: the person and environment depend on separate visual treatments to read. Scenes are not categorically banned. They are welcome if intelligently composed as self-contained, single-fill graphic units. In general, independent symbols, creatures, heads, objects, emblems, abstract forms, decorative clusters, ornaments, and graphic poster-like motifs are reliable starting points.

## Nine-design variation

When asked for a sheet, make nine distinct designs, not just small alterations of one item, unless the theme explicitly calls for a matched series. Vary silhouettes, orientation, internal structure, motif, and density. Mix bold forms with airy, highly detailed forms where appropriate; don’t force all glyphs into the same silhouette or symmetry.

## Theme and source handling

Use any user-supplied theme, text, images, references, style notes, or concepts as the subject matter and inspiration for the nine designs. Keep the format and extraction principles intact unless the user explicitly changes them. Do not invent a GitHub-repository ingestion workflow; that is a separate, future addendum.

## Explicit override policy

These are defaults, not restrictions on the user. If asked for a single glyph, more/fewer than nine, alternative aspect ratio, borders, mirrored variants, extra-large motifs, different color rules, or another layout, follow the override. Do not reinterpret a clear override as an error.

## Final visual checklist

Before finalizing, verify:

1. One square image; exactly nine designs in a loose invisible 3×3.
2. Genuine, generous, empty whitespace between every design horizontally, vertically, and diagonally; nothing joins rows or columns.
3. Black marks on a pure white ground only; no added faux finish.
4. Every visual detail belongs unambiguously to one of the nine motifs.
5. Each glyph works with one common finish applied to all its black areas.
6. Internal details and separation survive visually without oversimplification.
7. The designs look interesting, distinct, and appropriate to the supplied theme.

## Example invocation

> Make me a Daddy Dingy dingbat sheet of psychedelic cosmic tiaras, dripping stars, and impossibly fancy alien jewelry.

Apply this skill automatically to similar requests unless the user asks for another output format.
